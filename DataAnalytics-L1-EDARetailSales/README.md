## Key Findings

The analysis identified several important patterns:

1. **Electronics is the main revenue-driving category**, contributing approximately 52.1% of total revenue.

2. The **25–34 age group is the largest customer segment**, contributing approximately 38.4% of orders.

3. **November 2025 recorded the strongest monthly sales performance**, indicating a significant seasonal sales increase.

4. **Notebook Set** was the leading product by units sold.

5. Higher discount levels are associated with lower average profit per order, highlighting the importance of controlled discounting.

---

## Business Recommendations

### 1. Prioritize High-Revenue Categories

Maintain sufficient inventory and supplier capacity for high-performing categories such as Electronics while exploring cross-selling opportunities.

### 2. Target the Largest Customer Segment

Develop personalized campaigns and product bundles for the 25–34 customer segment while testing different offers for other age groups.

### 3. Use Discounts Strategically

Set discount limits according to product margins. Higher discounts should only be used when the expected increase in sales volume can compensate for lower profit per order.

### 4. Prepare for Seasonal Demand

Use monthly and quarterly sales trends to plan inventory, marketing budgets, and promotional campaigns before high-demand periods.

### 5. Evaluate Products Using Multiple Metrics

Product performance should be evaluated using quantity sold, revenue, and profit rather than relying on sales volume alone.

---

## Limitations

- The analysis is based on the available retail sales dataset.
- Correlation indicates association and does not prove causation.
- Sales volume, revenue, and profitability may lead to different conclusions about product performance.
- Discount analysis should be further validated using controlled experiments or additional business data.
